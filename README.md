# digital-archive-container
An open specification for preserving digital artifacts, their provenance, rights, experience requirements, and preservation context in a self-describing, portable container.

## Background

This project began with a fairly mundane problem: trying to identify old digital files accumulated across NAS storage and cloud drives.

Files can survive for decades while their context does not. A filename may tell us very little about what an artifact is, where it came from, which version it represents, what it depends on, what rights apply to it, or whether it can still be experienced.

The problem becomes more serious as digital services and software platforms disappear. An artifact may remain in someone's possession while the software, servers, authentication systems, distribution platforms, or other infrastructure required to use it are shut down.

Digital preservation therefore is not simply a matter of keeping the bytes. We also need to preserve enough context to understand what those bytes are, where they came from, what may legally be done with them, and what is required to experience them.

DAC is an attempt to explore that problem through an open, portable specification.

## A Longer-Term Dream

One hope for DAC is that digital publishers and creators will eventually think beyond the lifetime of their current platforms.

When a product is created today, its publisher may be gone decades from now. The storefront may be gone. The servers may be gone. The authentication system may be gone. The software required to experience it may be obsolete.

What happens then?

DAC could give publishers a way to express, while they are still around, how they want their products to be preserved and experienced in the future.

They may choose to permit future offline use, migration, emulation, or archival copies. They may choose to preserve particular dependencies. They may choose to retain certain restrictions. Or they may simply document that the original experience was inherently dependent on infrastructure that was never intended to survive indefinitely.

The important thing is that the decision is made deliberately and recorded.

The dream is not that every digital artifact must remain usable forever.

The dream is that **the people creating digital artifacts today can have a say in how those artifacts are understood and experienced long after the organizations and technologies that created them are gone.**
